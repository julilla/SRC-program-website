Responsible computing, taught where the code is written

Through the Socially Responsible Computing Teaching Assistant (STA) program, specially trained undergraduate TAs are assigned to individual courses and work closely with course staff to develop content connecting the technical material in a class to its social context.

The goal of the STA program is to make critical thought about the social impact of technical systems an integral part of the design and development process. The purpose of embedding SRC content throughout the CS curriculum, rather than in a standalone course, is to repeatedly expose students to the social, political, and ethical aspects of computing and directly relate these issues to technical choices. STAs are a regular part of the course staff, which enables them to contribute to both the technical and non-technical course content and help shape it to reflect the learning objectives outlined in the Socially Responsible Computing curriculum.

Goals for Students

Every course the STA program works with helps students build three habits of mind.

Technology is not neutral

Recognize that technology is not neutral, nor does it exist in a vacuum; it contains built-in biases that reflect the preferences, norms, and worldview of its creators.

Build with everyone in mind

Design and build systems that work for the full range of people they affect, not only the people who build them.

Branch out

Understand when to connect with other disciplines and stakeholders to build technologies in light of complex societal issues.

What STAs Do

Each course that opts into the STA program is assigned one or two STAs (or joint UTA-STAs). Courses touch on responsible computing multiple times during the semester, with coverage throughout the semester rather than as an add-on at the end.

Make the connections

Use the SRC curriculum and SRC handbook to understand societal issues relevant to the course, study its structure, and develop a plan for integrating social, political, and ethical material.

Create and improve content

Create and fine-tune SRC content with course staff for lectures, sections, labs, homework, and larger technical projects.

Implement content

Work with course staff to deploy SRC content during the semester: provide feedback to students, grade or guide the grading of SRC assignments, run discussion sections, and collect data to improve the content over time.

Work as a cohort

Meet as a group throughout the semester to discuss learning goals and grading practices, review each other’s content, and plan program-wide changes.

Every course is different

The role of the STA varies by course, depending on the maturity of the SRC content and the preferences of instructors and course staff. Some courses have well-established content that has been refined over several semesters, while courses that recently joined the program may have little or no existing content. Some professors already have well-developed ideas for content, assignments, and activities that they ask STAs to implement. Others rely on STAs to work with the SRC team to develop content.

Who Should Apply?

The program is looking for students with a genuine interest in aspects of socially responsible computing, who want to work closely with course staff to develop content connecting the technical material in their class to its social context.

The program doesn’t expect STA applicants to be experts, but as an applicant, you should have engaged with dimensions of socially responsible computing in the past. You should feel ready to expand and refine your knowledge, and share it with others. If this sounds like you, please apply! If you are still wondering if becoming an STA is right for you, feel free to get in touch with the Head STAs (HSTAs) at hstas@lists.cs.brown.edu or with Julia Netter.

  • Brown undergraduate or Master’s student, currently enrolled
  • Any concentration. You don’t have to concentrate in CS, though some courses require STAs to be familiar with the course material
  • Interest in honing pedagogical skills. Show why you are interested in developing your teaching abilities

How to Apply

Applications go through the CS department’s TA hiring process

STAs are hired for a specific course for one semester. Hiring starts early in the semester before the course is offered and runs in parallel with UTA hiring. The CS department’s TA hiring page has the current timeline, deadlines, and the application form when applications are open.

The hiring process

  1. 1

    Submit your application

    Apply through the CS department’s TA hiring page while applications are open.

  2. 2

    Pre-interview questionnaire

    Once applications have closed, the Head STAs will contact you with a questionnaire about your SRC-related interests and skills, and how you would apply them concretely in one of the courses you applied to. Plan to spend around 30 minutes on it: your answers are the basis for your interview, but they can and should be concise.

    Some (not all) courses ask you to submit their own questionnaire as well. You need to complete both the course-specific and the general STA questionnaire for your application to proceed. It is fine if your answers are similar. Look out for messages from both the Head STAs and the staff of the courses you applied to.

  3. 3

    Interview

    You will sign up for an interview with the SRC program team (the Head STAs and program director). It focuses on your SRC-related experience and skills, so be prepared to talk in more detail about your questionnaire answers.

    Some courses conduct additional, separate interviews; their HTAs will contact you to schedule them. For those courses, you must attend both the course-specific interview and the general STA interview.

Combined UTA-STA positions

Many courses hire joint UTA-STAs. To be eligible for those positions, you must apply both as a UTA and as an STA. Joint UTA-STAs can work up to 15 hours per week and are expected to spend about half their time on UTA work and half their time on STA work.

Frequently Asked Questions

In 2019, the Brown CS department started the “Responsible CS” initiative to integrate ethics and social impact topics broadly across its curriculum. The STA program, formerly called the Ethics Teaching Assistant (ETA) program, was the first step in this initiative.

Our focus on socially responsible computing is broader than ethics: we include a combination of understanding power and technology, making ethical design decisions, building accessible systems, and testing systems for (un)desirable impacts on various stakeholders. We want students to make decisions based on social, historical, and personal perspectives that are not necessarily part of traditional “ethics” frameworks.

For each course the program works with, one or more STAs work as members of the course staff to integrate SRC content into the course. Integrating content is a collaborative effort between the STAs, professors, and the rest of the course staff.
